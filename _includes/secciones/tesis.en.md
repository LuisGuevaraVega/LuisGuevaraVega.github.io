## The question

When Peru's higher-education regulator (SUNEDU) grants or denies a university its license, does the probability that its graduates land a job that matches their degree, is formal and pays better actually change?

  <div class="numero"><b>−4.6 pp</b><span>probability of informal employment for those same graduates</span></div>
  <div class="numero"><b>+44.3 pp</b><span>probability of non-professional employment when the license is denied</span></div>
</div>

## How I measured it

- **Data:** ENAHO household survey 2014–2022, university graduates.
- **Strategy:** a **difference-in-differences** estimator and an **instrumental variables** model, with time and university fixed effects. The treatment is licensing.
- **Theory:** signaling (Spence, 1973) and human capital (Becker, 1962). The license works as a quality signal for employers.

## Conclusion

Regulation does affect occupational mismatch, and **denial weighs more than approval**. The license shapes graduates' labor-market entry, but on its own it does not guarantee better perceived quality or productivity.
